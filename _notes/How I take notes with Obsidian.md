- **Daily notes.** I create a new daily note, every day. This is my workspace for the day. Everything gets saved here: meeting notes, social posts, articles for future review, tentative content ideas. I treat these daily notes as my inbox. Everything in here is treated as temporary, awaiting further triage where I will either commit it to a permanent note or, more likely, delete it. Each month I review all my daily notes, delete those that no longer interest me, and archive the remaining few in a folder named for the month and year. When something consequential happens, I save a one-sentence summary of the day in the YAML front-matter to make it easier to revisit that daily note in the future.
-  **To dos**. I have a single checkbox list of to dos that I keep pinned in my righthand sidebar. Each week, I create a new list of to dos, combining the week's priorities with any lingering items from last week's note (assuming I still care about them).
- **Links, not tags or folders**. I find tagging incredibly stressful and greatly prefer the freedom of linking.
- **Hub notes/MOCs.** If I find myself linking to a single note time and time again, I consider turning it into a "hub" note (often called "maps of content"). This is my only use for graph view: seeing notes with many connections.

- **Bases.**
- **Templates/frontmatter.** I have a handful of template notes with preconfigured YAML frontmatter. I use a frontmatter template that I insert into most new notes. It's simple, with a handful of fields, the most important of which is "hub".

![[Pasted image 20260318142820.png]]
- **Random notes.** Every day I aim to read a handful of random notes (using the Random note core plugin). This is my main mechanism for finding old ideas and making my vault more useful. Old ideas that no longer interest me are deleted without a second thought. I link orphan notes to other ideas, and sometimes, consolidate related notes together into a single, more useful note.
- **Plugins.** I use a minimal number of community plugins:
	- Excalidraw: sketching graphics for my writing and presentations.
	- Copilot: using an LLM directly in my notes (for example, filling out recipe cards).
	- Git: backing up my notes.
- **Mobile.**
- **Simplicity.**  I try to make my Obsidian setup simpler and simpler over time, simplifying processes and deleting plugins. As a general rule, the simpler my note-taking process, the more useful I find it.
- **Chaos.** My notes are a chaotic mess and I wouldn't have it any other way. I believe that laborious categorisation inhibits creativity, forcing you to conform new ideas to the small handful o available lens
